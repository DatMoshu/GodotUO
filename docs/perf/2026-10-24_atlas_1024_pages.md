# 1024 pages for the dynamic atlases (atlas split B)

Split from the bundled atlas change (#157) at the owner's request: this is
one of its three parts, on its own toggle. Off by default. The two-page
upload budget is not part of this change; it stays in #157, off.

## Problem

Godot replaces a whole texture on every `ImageTexture.Update`, so a page a
new sprite lands on is re-sent in full at the next flush. The lane's runtime
attribution counted 147 whole 2048x2048 page uploads (16 MiB each) in a 60 s
walk, 380 ms of summed upload calls.

## Change (`GUO_ATLAS_PAGE_1024=1`)

Layer: L0 renderer seam, `PORT DEVIATION (GUO)`, behind an environment
toggle; no profile field or Options entry until the owner decides.

The atlases that fill while walking (art, animation, gump, glyph) are marked
dynamic and, with the toggle on, cap their pages at 1024x1024: 4 MiB per
whole-page upload instead of 16. Land, texmaps and lights keep 2048 (merged
land's layers assume a 2048 page). Packing is upstream's; a sprite lands on a
different page and position, every UV is still an exact texel and sampling
stays nearest. A sprite wider or taller than 1022 pixels, which fitted a 2048
page, now gets a texture of its own, as anything over 2046 always did.

## What it may cost

The atlas probe's own table (on `TextureAtlas`) measured one upload at
1,676 us for 1024 against 1,909 us for 2048: a quarter of the bytes is not a
quarter of the time. Four times as many pages also means more texture
switches and possibly more draw calls. The measurement must show the walk's
upload time, page count, texture switches and draw calls, not bytes alone.
